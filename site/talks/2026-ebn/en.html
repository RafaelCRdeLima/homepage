Good afternoon. The question of this seminar fits in one line: the oscillation phase is the integral of p·dx along the path — and when the path is a geodesic in a curved spacetime, that integral changes. The trajectories in the background are exactly that: packets changing colour (flavour) while the well bends the path and changes the pace of the phase.

I

Part one

Neutrinos

A particle postulated out of desperation, detected 26 years later, which then spent three more decades “going missing” on its way from the Sun to us.

1914 – 1930 · the crisis

The beta spectrum doesn't add up

Fermi allowed spectrum, illustrative (Q = 1.16 MeV). Chadwick (1914) saw the continuum; Ellis & Wooster (1927) measured the energy calorimetrically: no energy was hiding in γ rays.

In a two-body decay n → p + e⁻, the electron would always come out with the same energy.

Instead it comes out with any energy between zero and Q. Energy and angular momentum seemed to vanish.

Niels Bohr went as far as considering that energy conservation might hold only on average in the nuclear world.

This is the starting problem. Two bodies in the final state, fixed energy. Experiment shows a continuum. Bohr was ready to give up energy conservation.

4 December 1930

“Liebe Radioaktive Damen und Herren”

“…a desperate remedy to save the law of conservation of energy.”
Pauli, letter to the participants of the Tübingen meeting

A neutral particle, spin ½, small mass, emitted together with the electron. He called it the “neutron”.

He didn't go to Tübingen: he preferred a ball in Zurich. He only published the idea in 1933.

The famous letter. Pauli did not even attend the meeting; he sent the letter. He himself thought the particle might never be detected.

1934 · Fermi

The neutrino gets a name and a theory

With Chadwick's neutron (1932) taking the name, Fermi rebaptises Pauli's particle: neutrino, “the little neutral one”.

n → p + e− + ν̄econtact interaction, constant GF

Rejected

Nature turned the paper down: “speculations too remote from reality”. It appeared in Zeitschrift für Physik.

Bethe & Peierls, 1934

σ ≲ 10−44 cm². An MeV neutrino would cross light-years of lead: “no practically possible way of observing the neutrino”.

Fermi's theory is the first theory of the weak interaction. And the Bethe–Peierls estimate explains why it took so long: the cross section is ridiculously small.

1956 · Savannah River

Detected, 26 years later

A reactor is a colossal source of antineutrinos: ~1013 cm−2 s−1 near the core. They looked for inverse beta decay:

ν̄e + p → e+ + ndelayed signal: positron annihilates at once · neutron captured μs later
“We are happy to inform you that we have definitely detected neutrinos… Observed cross section agrees well with expected six times ten to minus forty-four square centimeters.”
Telegram to Pauli, 14 June 1956
The delayed-coincidence signature was the key to beating the background. Pauli replied: everything comes to him who knows how to wait.

The number of light neutrinos coupling to the Z. The Standard Model has exactly three families — and, in it, massless neutrinos.

Keep the last sentence in mind: in the original Standard Model, neutrinos are massless. That is what oscillations will break.

The most intense source we have

The Sun is a νe factory

Only νₑ are produced. Measuring this flux tests, at once, the nuclear physics of the Sun and the physics of the neutrino.

Important for Part III: neutrinos leave the solar core directly; the energy carried by photons takes tens of thousands of years. Neutrinos are messengers from the interior.

The Standard Solar Model

The spectrum predicted by Bahcall

Fluxes from the B16-GS98 Standard Solar Model (Vinyoles et al. 2017); simplified beta spectral shapes, for illustration only. Bands: threshold of each technique.

Each experiment sees a different slice:

  • Gallium (0.23 MeV): sees the pp
  • Chlorine (0.81 MeV): ⁷Be and ⁸B
  • Water (≳ 3.5 MeV): ⁸B only, but in real time and with direction

John Bahcall spent 40 years refining this prediction.

The canonical plot. What matters are the thresholds: each technique sees a piece of the spectrum, which later allows separating solar physics from neutrino physics.

1968 – 1994 · Homestake

Ray Davis and the tank 1,478 m underground

615 t of perchloroethylene (dry-cleaning fluid). Every few months the argon atoms produced were extracted: about one every two days.

Result of 25 years: 2.56 ± 0.23 SNU. The prediction was ~8 SNU.

Two thirds were missing.

Counting individual argon atoms in a 615-tonne tank. A heroic experiment. And the result went 30 years without an accepted explanation.

The solar neutrino problem

Helioseismology confirmed the solar interior to ~0.1%. And the missing fraction depended on energy: no temperature tweak could explain the three experiments together.

1957 – 1968 · Pontecorvo, Maki, Nakagawa, Sakata

What if the neutrino changed flavour?

If the flavour states (the ones that interact) are not the mass states (the ones that propagate), each component accumulates phase at its own pace:

|να⟩ = Σk U*αk |νk⟩
Φk ≈ mk2 L2E
P(να→νβ) = sin²2θ · sin²Δm² L4Etwo flavours · vacuum · flat spacetime

In 1968, before Davis's result, Pontecorvo predicted the solar flux would arrive reduced by half.

Keep the phase Φ_k = m²L/2E in mind: it is the flat-space approximation of ∫p·dx. In Part III we will compute this same phase along a geodesic. And note the words in small print: vacuum, flat spacetime.

1998 · Super-Kamiokande

The νμ that cross the Earth disappear

50 kt of ultrapure water 1,000 m underground, 11,146 photomultipliers. Cherenkov light tells flavour and direction.

Atmospheric neutrinos from above travelled ~15 km; those from below, ~13,000 km. Same flux at production, by symmetry. Those from below arrive halved. Setting sin²2θ to zero shows the flat line that was expected. Kajita presented this in Takayama in June 1998.

2001 – 2002 · Sudbury Neutrino Observatory

The total flux was there

1,000 t of heavy water 2 km underground. The deuteron allows three reactions:

CC νₑ + d → p + p + e⁻ νₑ only
NC νx + d → p + n + νx all, equally
ES νx + e⁻ → νx + e⁻ all, νₑ weighs more

1σ bands from SNO, PRL 89, 011301 (2002). Without oscillation, the point would sit on the horizontal axis.

The three bands cross at a point far from the axis: two thirds of the νₑ arrive as νμ or ντ. And the total, the NC band, agrees with the Solar Model. Bahcall was right about the Sun.

Wolfenstein 1978 · Mikheyev & Smirnov 1985

Matter also changes oscillation

Electrons in the medium forward-scatter νₑ through the charged current. The other flavours do not feel this term:

VCC = √2 GF neeffective potential for νₑ

When 2√2 GF ne E ≈ Δm² cos 2θ, the effective mixing becomes maximal: the MSW resonance. In the Sun it converts ⁸B νₑ almost adiabatically into ν₂.

Keep for Part III

The potential acts in the local frame of the medium, with the neutrino's local energy.

In a supernova or a collapsar disk, density, energy and neutrino directions are measured next to a compact object — where “local” and “at infinity” are no longer the same thing.

I won't go into MSW details; it is enough that matter enters as a potential. Highlight the card: potential and energy are local quantities. That will be central in Part III.

Nobel Prize in Physics 2015

“…which shows that neutrinos have mass”

“for the discovery of neutrino oscillations, which shows that neutrinos have mass.”
Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences

Then came KamLAND (reactors, 2002), T2K, Daya Bay and RENO (θ₁₃, 2012), NOvA… The three-flavour picture closes.

Two experiments, two sources, one conclusion: oscillation, hence mass.

The Standard Model has to grow

What we still don't know

Six open questions

Ordering

Is ν₃ the heaviest or the lightest? DUNE, JUNO, Hyper-K.

CP violation

Is the phase δCP ≠ 0, π? It may be behind the matter–antimatter asymmetry (leptogenesis).

θ₂₃ octant

θ₂₃ < 45° or > 45°? The data can't tell yet.

Absolute scale

KATRIN: mν < 0.45 eV (2025). Cosmology (DESI + CMB): Σm ≲ 0.07 eV, model dependent.

Dirac or Majorana

Only neutrinoless ββ decay can tell: LEGEND, nEXO, KamLAND-Zen, SNO+…

Gravity

Every measurement assumes flat spacetime. What if the source sits in a deep gravitational well? Part III.

The sixth question is the bridge to the rest of the seminar. It's not on the “official list”, but it is where compact-object physics comes in.

An ultra-relativistic neutrino follows, to first order, the same null geodesic. Mass enters as a correction ∝ m²/E² — and that correction is exactly what carries the oscillation phase.

Compact objects

The zoo where only GR works

The EHT rings have diameters close to 2√27 GM/c², the shadow of the photon sphere. Neutron stars are the other extreme: matter at supranuclear densities, with r_s/R around 0.3.

Strong gravity, measured

Binary pulsars and gravitational waves

Two tests in the strong-field, dynamical regime. LIGO saw the merger of two black holes 1.3 billion light-years away.

Astrophysics · Woosley 1993

Collapsars: when a star fails to explode

A rapidly rotating Wolf–Rayet star of ~30 M☉ that has lost its hydrogen envelope.

  • The iron core collapses and the shock does not revive: a black hole of ~2–3 M☉ forms.
  • The envelope has too much angular momentum to fall straight in: an accretion disk forms a few rs out.
  • Accretion at 0.01–1 M☉/s for tens of seconds → relativistic jets that drill through the star.

WR 124, imaged by JWST in 2023: the kind of star that may end this way.

The collapsar is the standard model for long gamma-ray bursts. Woosley 1993, MacFadyen and Woosley 1999. Evidence: GRB 980425 with supernova 1998bw.

MacFadyen & Woosley 1999 · Popham, Woosley & Fryer 1999

An engine cooled by neutrinos

In the inner disk, matter is so hot and dense that photons are trapped. What carries the heat away are neutrinos: an NDAF (neutrino-dominated accretion flow).

And these neutrinos are emitted a few rs from a spinning black hole, where the geometry is Kerr, not Minkowski.

This is the link to Part III: the neutrino-emitting region of a collapsar sits in the full-GR zone. Light doesn't get out of there directly; neutrinos do.

Stodolsky 1979 · Fornengo, Giunti, Kim & Song 1997

The phase along the geodesic

Φk = ∫ pμ(k) dxμcovariant phase: valid in any metric
Φk = mk22E∞ ∫ dr√(1 − b²(1 − rs/r)/r²)Schwarzschild, first order in m², along the null geodesic

One-line derivation: with E and J = Eb conserved, Et − Jφ − ∫prdr cancels everything except the mass term.

1 · Radial: gravity “hides”

Φ = Δm²(rB − rA)/2E∞, the same form as flat space. But in local quantities (proper distance ∫dr/√B, energy Elocal) the flat formula is wrong.

2 · Non-radial: the path matters

The phase depends on b. Different paths between the same source and detector accumulate different phases.

3 · Many paths: interference

In a lens, the phase difference between paths is ∝ mk², not just Δm²: gravity gives access to the absolute mass.

It is the same phase as in Part I, m²L/2E, now written covariantly. Three consequences, which the next two dashboards show.

Dashboard · Swami, Lochan & Dixit 2020

A gravitational lens weighs the neutrino

interactive
normal orderinginverted

Source at 10 kpc, lens halfway. Two paths, b₊ and b₋. The common phase, ~m²L/E, is huge and washes out with any energy resolution. The difference between paths survives:

ΔΦk = mk2 Δb24E (1rA + 1rB)

Each mass eigenstate makes its own fringe. Change the lightest mass: the pattern changes, and the ordering leaves a mark too.

Point lens, weak field, no matter; amplitudes ∝ √|μ±|.

The physical message: ordinary oscillation only sees Δm². With two paths, interference sees m² of each state. The numbers are realistic: with a ~10 M☉ lens, the effective path difference is tens of km, and the phase is of order one or more for masses of tens of meV and supernova energies.

The frontier

What gravity can tell us

Absolute mass

Interference between lensed paths is sensitive to mk² and to the ordering (Fornengo et al. 1997; Swami et al. 2020).

Flavour near the source

MSW and collective conversions in local quantities; bent rays change the “halo” and fast flavour instabilities (Cherry et al. 2012).

Black-hole formation

The neutrino signal of a supernova that turns into a black hole is cut off abruptly: a clock for time-of-flight and mass measurements (Beacom, Boyd & Mezzacappa 2001).

Nucleosynthesis

In collapsar disks, the ν and ν̄ spectra that set Ye are shifted and focused by GR — changing heavy-element production (Caballero, McLaughlin & Surman 2012).

Equivalence principle

If different flavours felt gravity differently, oscillation would gain a term ∝ ΔγΦE (Gasperini 1988; Halprin & Leung 1991).

Decoherence

Wave packets of different masses separate; in strong fields, Shapiro delays and deflections also depend on mk.

Six research lines, several of them open. The first and the third have direct consequences for detectors like DUNE.

Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment

DUNE: 1,300 km from Fermilab to South Dakota

Far detector: 4 modules of ~17 kt of liquid argon (TPCs), 1.5 km underground. ProtoDUNE, at CERN, is the full-scale prototype.

Goals: δCP, ordering, octant — and supernova neutrinos.

The beam crosses the crust, with a matter effect that distinguishes the orderings. For the beam, Earth's gravity is irrelevant: DUNE is the flat reference laboratory. Curvature comes in when the source is a supernova.

A supernova in the Galaxy

DUNE is unique for νₑ (νₑ + ⁴⁰Ar → e⁻ + ⁴⁰K*): thousands of events for an SN at 10 kpc, with the neutronization peak and, if it happens, the cut-off of black-hole formation.

δ = −90° favours neutrinos in normal ordering: the two effects add up. In inverted ordering, they compete. Here spacetime is flat; the contrast with the astrophysical part is the point.

A network of observatories

Ears open for the next collapse

Together with DUNE, this network forms SNEWS: an automatic supernova alert. The next galactic supernova will be seen in tens of thousands of events, with millisecond resolution — enough to see the physics of the proto-neutron star, gravity included.

Summary

Three things to take home

1

Flavour oscillation is phase interference. The phase is ∫pμdxμ — and that holds in any geometry.

2

Supernovae and collapsars emit neutrinos a few rs out. There, redshift, bending and local quantities are not corrections: they are the physics.

3

Gravity doesn't only get in the way: with several paths, it measures mk². DUNE and the SNEWS network are ready for the next galactic supernova.

Recap the three ideas. Leave the question: what will the next galactic supernova tell us about neutrinos and gravity at the same time?

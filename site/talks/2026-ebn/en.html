Each node is a freely falling local frame, released from rest far away. Hamilton and Lisle’s river model. In Gullstrand–Painlevé coordinates, Schwarzschild is flat space flowing inward at the Newtonian escape velocity — exact in general relativity. Each node is a freely falling frame; animation time is their proper time. They never stop: they vanish on reaching the star and others enter at the edge. Near the mass they run faster, so the lattice deforms: stretched radially, squeezed sideways. At the surface of a 1.4 solar-mass neutron star, 0.59 c; the fall from 60 km takes 463 microseconds. Dragging rotates the lattice.

What we need from the metric

Curvature does two things to a neutrino

1 · It changes the energy

A neutrino emitted at r with local energy E arrives at infinity with

E∞ = E √(1 − rs/r)gravitational redshift, rs = 2GM/c²

The whole spectrum slides down, and the source's clock runs slower as seen from outside.

2 · It bends the path

The trajectory is a geodesic, not a straight line. Next to a compact object the deflection is large, and different paths can connect the same source to the same detector.

Photons and neutrinos follow the same geometry, which SN 1987A confirmed to ~0.5%.

Both matter because, in a supernova, emission happens deep in the well and detection happens far from it.

And there is a third, subtler one

The oscillation phase is accumulated along the geodesic, with the particle's proper time. In flat spacetime it reduces to the familiar Δm²L/4E; in a curved metric it does not.

We come back to this in three slides.

None of this is optional when the density that sets the MSW resonance is measured in the local frame and the detector sits 10 kpc away.

This is the slide that justifies the whole part to a neutrino audience: it is not relativity for its own sake, it is three concrete effects on what gets measured. Redshift on the spectrum, bending of the path, and the covariant phase.

The energy budget

The gravitational binding energy released is 3×1053 erg. Of that, 99% leaves as neutrinos, and only ~1051 erg becomes kinetic energy of the explosion.

A supernova is, first of all, a neutrino event. The light is loose change.

Before the table: a day of silicon burning builds the ~1.4 M☉ iron core. During infall, above 1012 g/cm³ the νe become trapped, and the bounce occurs at 2.7×1014 g/cm³.

Timings from the delayed mechanism of Bethe & Wilson (1985). Radii depend on the progenitor; these are typical of ~15 M☉.

Worth stressing the contrast of scales: collapse takes 200 ms, the bounce is instantaneous, the stalling lasts hundreds of milliseconds, and propagation takes hours. And the budget: 99% of the energy leaves as neutrinos. That is why a supernova is a neutrino laboratory before it is an optical event.

The delicate point of the mechanism

The shock stalls, and must be revived

The prompt shock does not blow up the star. It spends all its energy before getting out.

Photodisintegration

Breaking iron into nucleons costs 8.8 MeV per nucleon. Crossing 0.1 M☉ of iron consumes ~1.7×1051 erg, of the order of the shock's whole energy.

Neutrino losses

As it crosses the neutrinosphere, the freshly shocked matter becomes transparent and dumps the breakout burst. More energy gone.

Within ~20 ms it stops at ~150 km: it becomes a standing accretion shock.

What revives it

Between the neutrinosphere and the shock lies the gain region, where absorption of νe and ν̄e beats emission. A few per cent of the luminosity deposited there is enough to push the shock back out.

νe + n → p + e−  ·  ν̄e + p → n + e+the heating that reignites the explosion

But the deposition is marginal: in spherical symmetry it almost never suffices. What closes the budget is convection and the SASI, which hold matter longer in the gain region.

That is why the problem was only settled in 3D, and why every progenitor explodes at a different instant.

This slide explains why supernovae have been a hard problem for 60 years. The prompt shock fails; the mechanism is delayed and depends on multidimensional hydrodynamics. For us, what matters is the output: a real, sharp shock front that starts running outward at around 0.3 s.

Collapsars: when a star fails to explode

A rapidly rotating Wolf–Rayet star of ~30 M☉ that has lost its hydrogen envelope.

  • The envelope has too much angular momentum to fall straight in: an accretion disk forms a few rs out.

The frontier

What gravity can tell us

Absolute mass

Interference between lensed paths is sensitive to mk² and to the ordering (Fornengo et al. 1997; Swami et al. 2020).

Flavour near the source

MSW and collective conversions in local quantities; bent rays change the “halo” and fast flavour instabilities (Cherry et al. 2012).

Black-hole formation

The neutrino signal of a supernova that turns into a black hole is cut off abruptly: a clock for time-of-flight and mass measurements (Beacom, Boyd & Mezzacappa 2001).

Nucleosynthesis

In collapsar disks, the ν and ν̄ spectra that set Ye are shifted and focused by GR — changing heavy-element production (Caballero, McLaughlin & Surman 2012).

Equivalence principle

If different flavours felt gravity differently, oscillation would gain a term ∝ ΔγΦE (Gasperini 1988; Halprin & Leung 1991).

Decoherence

Wave packets of different masses separate; in strong fields, Shapiro delays and deflections also depend on mk.

Six research lines, several of them open. The first and the third have direct consequences for detectors like DUNE.

Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment

DUNE: 1,300 km from Fermilab to South Dakota

Far detector: 4 modules of ~17 kt of liquid argon (TPCs), 1.5 km underground. ProtoDUNE, at CERN, is the full-scale prototype.

Goals: δCP, ordering, octant — and supernova neutrinos.

The beam crosses the crust, with a matter effect that distinguishes the orderings. For the beam, Earth's gravity is irrelevant: DUNE is the flat reference laboratory. Curvature comes in when the source is a supernova.

The star offers every density between 1014 g/cm³ in the core and almost nothing in the envelope. Each resonance picks its own.

Both sit in the envelope, not in the core: the H resonance lies tens of thousands of kilometres from the centre, and the L one further still.

Only one of them meets the shock

The shock front takes seconds to sweep the envelope. When it passes 1.6×10³ g/cm³ it crosses the H resonance. By the time it reaches the 19 g/cm³ of the L, it is already weak and diffuse.

That is why the rest of this talk is about H.

Ye = 0.5. The density scales as 1/E: at 5 MeV the H resonance sits at 6.3×10³ g/cm³, and at 60 MeV at 5.3×10².

This slide answers the question the audience asks silently: why is the resonance where it is. The answer is that it is not at a radius, it is at a density, and the star offers all of them. And it explains why only H matters here.

The ruler behind the criterion

Adiabaticity, and the length that measures everything

At the resonance the two matter eigenstates nearly touch. If the neutrino crosses slowly, it follows the eigenstate and PH = 0. If it crosses fast, it hops, and PH → 1.

“Slow” and “fast” are measured against a single length, the oscillation length at the resonance:

Losc = 4πE / (Δm² sin 2θ₁₃)at the H resonance, at 20 MeV: 67.6 km

This is the ruler for the next slides

The criterion that a structure only perturbs the conversion if it is shorter than Losc is measured in this unit. And the front width recorded in M15-7b, 284 km, is exactly 4.2 Losc.

Conclusion 1 · the criterion

What the resonance can see

A structure perturbs the conversion only if it is shorter than Losc at the resonance. Below that, the effect grows as the square of the contrast — measured as the 2.02 ± 0.03 power — and saturates at 8%.

But in a simulation…

The real shock is centimetres wide. The file stores the width of the numerical scheme.

Measured: the two epochs of M15-7b (3 and 10 s). Model: the interpolation between them. Control: the front width, which no data provides. With the stored width, P_H sits at the adiabatic value; at the physical width it rises to ~0.5–0.6. Play shows the front sweeping through the resonance.

Conclusion 2 · the cost

Restoring the front removes half of PH

0.44–0.76

absolute ΔPH, six progenitors, 5 to 100 MeV (median 0.56)

The file as stored returns the adiabatic limit in 99.9% of rays. With the front restored, the spread (s.d. 0.32) is where one supernova is drawn from.

The closed form tracks each ray: Pearson 0.9987, no free parameter.

It does not perturb P_H: omitting the correction removes half of it. And we measured it on every line of sight, not only on average — a supernova is observed from one direction.

A bias of fixed sign could be absorbed into a correction factor. One whose sign follows the flavour hierarchy of the source cannot — and it is degenerate with the spectral quantities one wants to extract.

It survives in both orderings, for different reasons: −4.1% in normal, −5.4% in inverted. The systematic cannot be avoided by assuming an ordering.

The consequence at DUNE: a few per cent, but with a sign that changes between model families. That is what makes the effect dangerous: it is not a universal correction factor.

Conclusion 4 · where to look

The error lives in a band, in closed form

The error is not spread out: it occupies a band in the time–energy plane, and the band has a closed form.

It follows from the presupernova density profile, given the shock radius and the compression. With no flavour calculation at all.

And it closes: past t ≃ 4 s the shock has already left the density region the H resonance occupies at observable energies.

The strong point here is that the band is predictable in advance, from the structure of the star alone. Anyone looking for the effect knows where to look before running any flavour code.

Conclusion 4 · the trap

Every progenitor has its own band

Five sibling progenitors, at the same epoch, span 1.4 to 61.6 MeV. And no single energy falls inside all five bands.

The practical consequence

Comparing models at a fixed energy returns a false null: in each model you will be outside some other model's band.

We fell into that trap before measuring it.

The band depends on each star's presupernova structure, which differs between progenitors even at the same initial mass.

This is the most useful slide for anyone repeating the work: the energy where the effect shows up is not universal. Pick one energy, compare models, and you measure zero and conclude wrongly.

Honestly

What this settles and what it doesn't

Three statements

  • A criterion with a number: the response is extinguished above ≃ 3 Losc. “Steepen the front by hand” becomes a checkable condition.
  • The cost: 0.44–0.76 in PH and a few per cent in the argon count, with a sign set by the source.
  • Where to look: the (t, E) band in closed form, from presupernova structure.

Limits

  • The scales that act are in no model: grids resolve nothing shorter than 4.7 Losc. The turbulence amplitude is extrapolated.
  • Blue supergiants only (six Garching models).
  • Not yet an observable: a few σ at 10 kpc, scaling as 1/d.

R. C. R. de Lima, Which density structures affect supernova neutrino flavour conversion, and the shock front at the MSW resonance — in preparation (2026).

Three statements checkable against any model set, with no code of mine in the loop. And the limits, stated plainly.

Summary

Four things to take home

1

Flavour oscillation is phase interference. The phase is ∫pμdxμ — and that holds in any geometry.

2

Supernovae and collapsars emit neutrinos a few rs out. There, redshift, bending and local quantities are not corrections: they are the physics.

3

Gravity doesn't only get in the way: with several paths, it measures mk². DUNE and the SNEWS network are ready for the next galactic supernova.

4

In a supernova only the shock front passes the resonance criterion — and simulations store it too wide. Restoring it changes PH by 0.44–0.76 and the DUNE count by a few per cent.

Recap the three ideas. Leave the question: what will the next galactic supernova tell us about neutrinos and gravity at the same time?

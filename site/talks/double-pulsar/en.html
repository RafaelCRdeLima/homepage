The moment of inertia of pulsar A

Bayesian predictions for the moment of inertia of PSR J0737−3039A and tests of the strange-star hypothesis

Rafael C. R. de Lima

with João Vitor Zastrow and Jonas P. Pereira

UDESC/CCT · Joinville · UnB · Brasília

The double pulsar is the only known system in which two pulsars orbit each other, and its timing is expected to measure a moment of inertia. This seminar turns the question “can this pulsar be a strange star?” into an inference with public data. Manuscript in preparation; author list and affiliations still to be confirmed.

Both are measurements of the same mass, with relative errors of about 1×10⁻⁵ and 5×10⁻⁵. The periastron advance includes the Lense–Thirring term, which depends on I_A. The main solution estimates I_A from radius limits of another analysis (Dietrich et al. 2020) and a radius–I relation (Lattimer 2019), which builds in an equation-of-state assumption. For consistency we condition on the solution that uses k, s and the orbital decay simultaneously, with I_A free, at the cost of an error about five times larger. The numerical difference is irrelevant: computed for a typical strange star and for SLy, I_A changes by about 2×10⁻⁶ in relative terms. The point is logical.

Part II

Two families

Self-bound quark matter against a flexible nucleonic parameterization

Two parameterizations, with validated forward models. The strange family reduces to one shape parameter, and that changes the cost of the whole inference.

Self-bound

The strange-matter hypothesis

Ω = 34π² ( −a₄ μ⁴ + a₂ μ² ) + BGrand potential of quark matter · μ is the quark chemical potential
a₄ ≤ 1
perturbative QCD corrections
a₂
strange-quark mass and pairing
B
effective bag constant

If three-flavor matter is the ground state, a whole star can be made of it: self-bound, with finite density at the surface. We model a bare surface.

Alford et al. 2005 · Manuscript · §II
Same conventions as the previous paper. The squared sound speed never exceeds one in this form. The bare surface is an assumption; Part IV returns to it, because a thin nuclear crust would add a few hundred meters to the radius.

The stability window

Stable with three flavors, unstable with two

Bmax = g₀²(g₀²a₄ − 9a₂) / 108π²
Bmin = g₀²54π² [ 4g₀²a₄(1 + 21/3)³ − 3a₂ ]

g₀ = 930 MeV. Three-flavor matter must be more bound than ⁵⁶Fe, and two-flavor matter less: otherwise ordinary nuclei would convert.

The window exists for a₂ ≤ α a₄, with α ≈ (297 MeV)², and Bmin > 0 throughout it.

Prior domain in (a₂, B¼)interactive
Zastrow et al. 2025 · Manuscript · §II and §V
The shaded area is the absolute-stability window for the a₄ on the slider, cut to the range of the main prior: a₂ from minus three alpha to alpha a₄. The dashed lines are curves of constant λ, the parameter of the next slide. The lower limit of a₂ is arbitrary, and that arbitrariness returns when we talk about the sign of λ.

Homology

Three parameters, one shape

ε̃ = 3p̃ + 4 + 3λ²4π² [ 1 + sgn(λ) √( 1 + 16π²3λ² (p̃ + 1) ) ]    λ ≡ a₂√(a₄B)p = B p̃ and ε = B ε̃ · λ = 0 is the bag model · λ < 0 (a₂ < 0) gives cs² > 1/3

The dimensionless EOS knows only λ, and the structure equations have no other scale. At fixed λ, masses and radii scale as B−1/2; Λ, Ī and the compactness depend only on λ and pc/B.

Two consequences: the observables measure only (λ, B), so a₄ at fixed (λ, B) is informed only by the window; and the forward model becomes a one-parameter family, tabulated once.

Manuscript · §II · equivalent rescaling in Zhang & Mann 2021, here for both signs of a₂
This is the analytic result that makes the inference cheap. The expression is continuous at λ = 0. Zhang and Mann noted an equivalent rescaling for interacting quark matter. The homology was checked numerically with the three original parameters, without using the rescaling itself.

Likelihood

One population, marginalized masses

𝓛k(θ) = ∫ dM p( dk | M, X(M; θ) ) π(M | θ)     π(M | θ) = 1Mmax(θ) − MlowX is the radius or the deformability given by the EOS · Mlow = 1 M☉

The normalization penalizes EOSs whose maximum mass greatly exceeds the observed masses. That is a choice of population model, not a datum.

So there is a variant with an EOS-independent density, 1/(Mup − Mlow) on [Mlow, Mmax], with Mup = 3.5 M☉. In it, ln 𝓑 goes from +0.21 to +0.98.

Manuscript · §IV and §X
Each star whose mass is inferred from data gets a mass from the population, marginalized numerically. The same density holds for both components of GW170817. The variant stands for a population uniform up to 3.5 solar masses of which only the stars below M_max are neutron stars.

Mass and radius

NICER: the density already contains the radio mass

The priors of the X-PSI analyses are uniform in Req on [3rg(1 M☉), 16 km] and already contain the radio mass.

So the posterior density in (M, Req) is the X-ray likelihood times the radio measurement, divided by a constant. We use a KDE of it as p(d | M, R) and do not add those masses again.

Do the prior cuts matter?

Where the prior vanishes the estimate vanishes, which acts as an exclusion. For J0740 the binding cut is the compactness one, 3GM/c² = 9.2 km at 2.07 M☉.

It lies below the 0.1% points of R₂.₀ in both posteriors: 10.4 and 10.1 km. No posterior EOS approaches 16 km.

Salmi et al. 2024 · Choudhury et al. 2024 · Fonseca et al. 2021 · Manuscript · §IV
This was one of the points revised after the external audit. A published density is not automatically a likelihood: one has to state what is in it. Here it is X rays times radio, which is why the radio masses of J0740 and J0437 do not enter again. The KDE uses reflection at the 16 km edge.

A biased move

Every diagnostic passed, and the tail was wrong

The first nucleonic emcee run mixed DE (80%) and DE-snooker (20%). It passed every convergence test, but put 0.138 and 0.039 of the mass above the library's 84 and 95% points of IA, instead of 0.16 and 0.05.

That is 4.8 and 5.7 standard errors. Nested sampling agreed with the library.

# emcee 3.1.6, DESnookerMove
u = delta / sqrt(norm)
q = s + u * gamma * (dot(u, z1) − dot(u, z2))
return q, 0.5 * (ndim − 1.0) * metropolis

The step grows with the distance to the reference walker, and the Jacobian factor has half the exponent of ter Braak & Vrugt (2008). The move does not leave the target invariant.

Manuscript · §VI · ter Braak & Vrugt 2008 · emcee/moves/de_snooker.py, version 3.1.6
The lesson is methodological: R-hat and autocorrelation time measure mixing, not invariance of the target. Only the comparison with independent methods exposed the problem. All runs in the paper now use DE moves only; repeating the strange-star runs that way changed the numbers only within their Monte Carlo errors.

The test that exposes the bias

64 walkers, standard normal in 4 dimensions, scaled by σ

The same emcee moves run now in the browser. A correct target gives variance 1.

With 20 thousand steps: 0.73 with snooker alone, 0.94 in the 80/20 mixture and 1.002 with DE alone.

The bias depends on the units. The step scales with the parameters: for width 0.1 the move is accepted for 91% of the proposals, and for width 100 for 0.2%.

In (a₂ [MeV²], B¼ [MeV]) it was inert: 0 of 7200 proposals accepted. In the nucleonic parameters, it was not.

ln Z is −19.37 for strange stars and −19.58 for nucleonic stars. Monte Carlo errors are small, but the relevant uncertainty is that of the choices. We attach no meaning to a preference that changes sign when one pulsar is added. KDE bandwidths of ×0.7 and ×1.4 move ln B by less than 0.05.

Fit against prior volume

The sign comes from volume, not from fit

ln Z = ⟨ln 𝓛⟩post − DKL

Nucleonic stars fit better on average and at the best point: ⟨ln 𝓛⟩ = −16.77 against −17.44; maxima −15.61 and −16.31.

But their posterior occupies a smaller fraction of their prior: DKL = 2.8 against 1.9 nats.

The small positive ln 𝓑 comes from the concentration within the prior, not from a better description of the data.

Manuscript · §VIII
The decomposition is exact: evidence is mean fit minus information gained. Someone reading only the sign of ln B would conclude the opposite of what the fit shows. That is one more reason not to read +0.21 as a preference for strange stars.

Consistency between data sets

GW170817 pulls strange stars the other way

ln Rk = ln Z − ln Zk − ln Z∖k
strangenucleonic

Positive: the data set is more probable given the others than a priori. The radio masses and the two NICER pulsars are consistent: ln R between +0.7 and +1.3.

For nucleonic stars GW170817 is neutral, ln R = 0.0. For strange stars there is mild tension, ln R = −1.7: GW170817 alone prefers softer stars than the maximum mass and J0740 require.

Alone it would give ln 𝓑 = +0.8; without it, +1.1. The tension brings the combined value close to zero.

Manuscript · §VIII
The likelihood of each data set was stored for every prior draw, so the evidence of any subset comes without a new run. Note that two positive results combine to a value close to zero: that is the effect of the internal tension of the strange family.

In the overlap (IA = 1.35), even at 2%: median +0.2, with 90% of the realizations between −0.8 and +1.0.

From the nucleonic 5% point to the strange 95% point, the median |ln 𝓑| at 2% is at most 3.2: odds of about 25:1, for equal prior odds.

At 10%, the precision anticipated around 2030, the median stays below 1.2 in all cases.

Statements about the simulated cases. True values beyond these points, improbable under both families, would give larger |ln 𝓑|.

Manuscript · §IX · illustrations of information content, not instrumental forecasts
Importance reweighting with 2×10⁷ prior draws for strange stars, because a simulated I_A in the tail selects few MCMC samples; the effective sample size is at least 238 in every case. The strongest case is a true I_A at the nucleonic 5% point, with ln B = −3.2. That is a statement about the simulated cases, not a general limit.

Limitations

What is not in the model

1

Crust and atmosphere

Bare strange stars; a thin crust would add hundreds of meters. NICER assumes a hydrogen atmosphere and shapes calibrated on hadronic stars, which amounts to a thin crust.

2

Rotation

Non-rotating forward model. With second-order Hartle on 300 EOSs: J0740 gains up to 0.22 km (strange) and 0.32 km (nucleonic); J0437, up to 0.08 km, far below the NICER uncertainties. Mmax rises by up to 0.022 M☉.

3

The families themselves

No mixed binaries, no hybrid family, no phase transitions, no explicit chiral constraints. Discrimination refers to these two parameterizations.

Manuscript · §IV and §X
For rotation, we compare each rotating star with the static star of the same gravitational mass, including monopole and quadrupole sectors and the surface term of self-bound stars. For 95% of the EOSs the effect on J0740 stays below 0.16 km. The nucleonic solver failed at low central density for 18 of the 300 EOSs. The mass of J0740 could gain less than a third of its uncertainty at the upper limit of the integral; we neglect that.

Conclusions

A narrow prediction, an open evidence

1

IA = 1.44 (1.29–1.58), RA = 10.8 (10.1–11.4) km

Robust to the priors, resting on the NICER radii. The sign of λ stays where the prior put it.

2

Current data do not discriminate these families

ln 𝓑 of order unity, reflecting the concentration within the priors, not the quality of the fit.

3

GW170817 in mild tension within the strange family

With the maximum mass and the NICER radii. Not within the nucleonic family.

4

The information is in the (IA, RA) plane

IA alone at 2%: median |ln 𝓑| up to 3.2 in the simulated cases. A hypothetical independent radius with a 0.5 km error, for bare strange stars, would give |ln 𝓑| > 2 with the right sign in 62–64% in the overlap; no such measurement exists.

Conclusions conditional on the two parameterizations, the data selection and the assumptions of §X · Manuscript in preparation
Methodological close: the nucleonic results come from importance sampling, reproduced by nested sampling and by emcee, and that comparison exposed a bias of the DE-snooker move of emcee 3.1.6 that the usual diagnostics do not reveal. Anyone using that move with parameters of small width should repeat the test.
